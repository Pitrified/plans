# /tmp as a RAM-backed tmpfs

Status: open, no decision. The user chose to wait (2026-11-17).

## Goal

Decide whether `/tmp` stays a tmpfs on this box.

## Findings

- `/tmp` is Ubuntu 26.04's default, not a local choice: systemd's stock `tmp.mount` (`/usr/lib/systemd/system/tmp.mount`, systemd 259), enabled by Ubuntu since 25.10.
  Options `size=50%` (16 GB here), `nr_inodes=1m`, `usrquota`. Nothing in `/etc/fstab` or `/etc/systemd/system/` overrides it.
- Files in `/tmp` live in RAM and go to swap under pressure.
  Claude Code's scratchpad is under `/tmp/claude-1000/`, so scratch work lands there.
- 2026-11-17: an 8.9 GB scratch Unity clone in the scratchpad plus an idle 6 GB Gradle daemon filled swap, and Claude Code stopped an APK build for low memory.
  After deleting the clone, `shared` went from about 7 GB to 139 MB.
- A second Unity clone in the scratchpad failed to import packages with error -122 (`EDQUOT`, the per-user quota).

## Options

- **a. Put `/tmp` on disk.** `sudo systemctl mask tmp.mount`, then reboot; Ubuntu's documented opt-out.
  `systemd-tmpfiles` still ages files out of `/tmp` (10 days by default).
  Rollback: `sudo systemctl unmask tmp.mount`, reboot.
- **b. Keep the tmpfs, smaller.** A drop-in `/etc/systemd/system/tmp.mount.d/size.conf` with `[Mount]` `Options=mode=1777,strictatime,nosuid,nodev,size=4G,nr_inodes=1m,x-systemd.graceful-option=usrquota`, then reboot.
  Large writes then fail on the cap instead of using RAM.
  Rollback: delete the drop-in, reboot.
- **c. Change nothing.** Keep Unity projects, builds and other large trees out of `/tmp` (on disk, e.g. under `~/.cache/`), session by session.

Any of a or b needs sudo, through the sudo-handoff skill, and a reboot timed by the user.
